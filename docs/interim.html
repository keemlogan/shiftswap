1. Introduction

This interim report specifies the requirements of ShiftSwap and presents the UML models used to analyse them. ShiftSwap is a web application for a small store with part-time workers. It manages the weekly shift schedule, finds substitutes when a worker cannot work a shift, and computes weekly holiday allowance and monthly pay under Korean labor law.

The proposal (Week 5) defined the problem, the goals, the nine main functions and the project plan. This report refines them: problem (Section 2), elicitation (3), functional and non-functional requirements (4, 5), scenarios (6), analysis models (7), main screens (8) and validation (9). The team follows the incremental model with prototyping of the user interface (Chapter 1): increment 1 delivers the Must use cases and increment 2 the Should use cases. The screens in Section 8 come from the prototype, which we use to validate requirements with the owner and workers before the design is fixed. All identifiers (UC, FR, NFR, BR, TC) come from one project specification that the team uses as the single source of truth; the same identifiers appear in the models, the code and the tests.

2. Problem description

2.1 Background and current process

In small stores (cafés, convenience stores, restaurants) with fewer than ten part-time workers, each worker has fixed weekly shifts, for example every Monday 18:00–23:00. When a worker cannot come, the owner phones or messages other workers one by one to find a substitute. The owner then edits a spreadsheet by hand. At month end the owner must re-check each worker's weekly hours to decide whether the weekly holiday allowance (주휴수당) is owed.

Our reference store is the workplace of team member Hyewon Kim. The owner also acts as the manager. There, finding a substitute typically takes 3 to 5 contacts per request. The current process has four manual steps: (1) the worker informs the owner; (2) the owner contacts other workers one at a time; (3) the owner edits the spreadsheet and informs both workers; (4) at month end the owner re-counts hours and computes pay. None of these steps is recorded except the final spreadsheet.

Korean law adds rules that depend on these hours. A worker whose weekly contractual hours are 15 or more and who worked every scheduled day of the week is entitled to a paid weekly holiday (Labor Standards Act Art. 55 ①, Art. 18 ③, Enforcement Decree Art. 30 ①) [3, 5]. The 2026 minimum hourly wage is KRW 10,320 [1, 2]. Overtime and night premiums apply only in workplaces with five or more regular employees (Art. 11, 56) [3]. The minimum wage may be reduced to 90 % during probation only if several conditions hold (Minimum Wage Act Art. 5 ②, Enforcement Decree Art. 3) [4].

2.2 Problems P1–P3

P1 Finding a substitute costs the owner repeated one-to-one contacts (reference store: typically 3–5 contacts per request).

P2 The schedule, weekly hours and holiday-allowance eligibility of two workers change with every swap, and all recalculation is manual.

P3 Legal pay rules (minimum wage, holiday allowance, 5-employee threshold for premiums, probation reduction) are applied from memory, so errors are not caught before payday.

Existing shift-scheduling products (Shiftee, the work-management features of Albamon and Alba Cheonguk, When I Work, Homebase, Deputy) manage schedules and shift trades. None of those we examined links substitute approval to Korean holiday-allowance eligibility at the moment of approval [10].

2.3 Proposed solution and system environment

ShiftSwap replaces steps (2) to (4). A worker creates a substitute request for one of their shifts. The system selects every eligible colleague and puts the request in their inbox at once. The first colleague to accept becomes the acceptor. The owner sees what the swap does to both workers' hours and holiday-allowance eligibility, and approves or rejects it. On approval the schedule and weekly summaries are updated. At month end the system produces a payroll draft and flags minimum-wage problems.

The system runs entirely in a web browser. It is a static web application (HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules) with an embedded SQLite database (sql.js, compiled to WebAssembly) [9]. Data is saved in the browser's local storage after every transaction. There is no server and no installation. The application is published on GitHub Pages. The same device can be used by the owner on a laptop and by workers on phones, because every screen works at 360 px and 1440 px width. Because the application is a demonstration system without a server, sign-in is done by choosing a demo account, and the passage of time is simulated by an adjustable demo clock. The default demo time is Monday 2026-09-28 09:00.

The demo data describes Dalbit Café (달빛카페), a fictional store with four regular employees and the default substitution-attendance policy EXCUSED. It is used in all scenarios and screenshots of this report.

2.4 Actors

The system has three actors. A1 Worker (primary, human) is a part-time employee who views their own schedule, registers availability, requests substitutes, responds to requests and records attendance. A2 Owner (primary, human) is the store owner, who also acts as manager: the owner registers workers and fixed schedules, approves or rejects substitutions, confirms attendance, reviews weekly summaries, confirms monthly payroll and edits settings. A3 System Clock (secondary, system) is a time-triggered actor. It expires unanswered or unapproved requests at their deadline and closes weeks for the weekly summary; in the demo application it is driven by the adjustable demo clock.

3. Requirements elicitation

Chapter 8 describes requirements elicitation as work with the stakeholders to find out about the application domain, the services the system should provide and its constraints. Our stakeholders are the store owner, the part-time workers and, indirectly, the labor law that fixes the pay rules. We use four sources.

  1. Domain experience. Hyewon Kim works part-time in the reference store. Her experience gave the problem statement and the figure of typically 3–5 contacts per substitute request.
  2. Interviews. Semi-structured interviews with the owner of the reference store and two of Hyewon's co-workers. Hyewon Kim leads each interview and a second member takes notes. Each interview is planned for about 30 minutes.
  3. Document analysis. The owner's schedule spreadsheet (structure only; no personal data is copied) and the legal sources on minimum wage, weekly holiday and probation [1–5].
  4. Prototype sessions. After the first interviews the owner and the co-workers are shown the UI prototype (Section 8) and asked to perform one task each.

Question 4 to the workers addresses the concurrency requirement (BR-02, NFR-04). Question 7 to the workers addresses privacy (NFR-11). The notes of each interview are kept by the requirements lead. This report does not include interview results beyond the problem statement; answers that change a requirement will be recorded in the final report together with the changed requirement.

4.2 Business rules

The business rules are the domain requirements (Chapter 8) that several functional requirements share. They are stated once here and referenced by ID.

Table 5. Business rules
IDRule
BR-01Eligible candidates. A worker W is eligible for a request on shift S (date d, start s, end e) iff: W is active; W ≠ requester; W has no shift on date d overlapping [s, e); W has an availability slot on weekday(d) with slotStart ≤ s and slotEnd ≥ e.
BR-02First acceptance wins. Acceptance succeeds only if the request is REQUESTED at the moment of the transaction; it sets request.status = ACCEPTED, request.acceptorId = W, target(W).response = ACCEPTED, and all other PENDING targets → CLOSED.
BR-03Request validity. Only the shift's current worker can request; the shift must be SCHEDULED and start in the future; at most one open (REQUESTED/ACCEPTED) request per shift; the deadline must be after now and no later than the shift start.
BR-04Approval effect. Approve: shift.workerId = acceptor; shift.originalWorkerId keeps the first worker (set only if null); request → APPROVED. Reject: request → REJECTED, shift unchanged. The owner can approve only an ACCEPTED request.
BR-05Week. A week runs Monday 00:00 to Sunday 24:00 (ISO week). Weekly records are keyed by the Monday date.
BR-06Holiday-allowance eligibility. Eligible iff (a) weekly contractual hours ≥ 15, and (b) perfect attendance: every shift of the week whose original worker is W and that was not given away was WORKED (not ABSENT). Shifts W gave away by an APPROVED substitution count according to Workplace.subAttendancePolicy: EXCUSED (default; the working day was changed by agreement, so it does not break attendance) or ABSENT (it breaks attendance). Contractual hours = sum of W's fixed-schedule hours per week. Substitute shifts W took do not change contractual hours. (The law averages 15 h over four weeks; the fixed schedule is constant, so the average equals the weekly value.)
BR-07Holiday-allowance hours. hours = min(contractualHours, 40) / 40 × 8, rounded to 2 decimals; pay = hours × effective hourly wage, rounded down to the won.
BR-08Premium pay. Applies only if Workplace.regularEmployees ≥ 5. Then +50 % of wage for (i) daily hours beyond 8, (ii) weekly hours beyond 40 not already counted in (i), (iii) night hours 22:00–06:00. The night premium adds to the overtime premium. If regularEmployees < 5, premium = 0.
BR-09Minimum-wage check. Warn if worker.hourlyWage < MinimumWage(year).hourly × (0.9 if BR-10 applies else 1). The payroll still computes with the worker's wage; the warning is shown on the payroll row and blocks confirmation until acknowledged.
BR-10Probation reduction. The minimum may be reduced to 90 % only if all hold: contract length ≥ 1 year (contractEnd − contractStart ≥ 365 days, or contractEnd empty = open-ended); the date is within 3 months of contractStart and ≤ probationEnd; simpleLabor = false.
BR-11Monthly payroll. Month = calendar month. Base pay = Σ actual hours of WORKED shifts in the month × wage (if no attendance is recorded yet, scheduled hours are used and the row is flagged “estimated”). Holiday allowance = Σ holiday pay of weeks whose Sunday falls in the month. Total = base + holiday + premium. Amounts are integers in KRW, rounded down.
BR-12Expiry. A REQUESTED or ACCEPTED request whose deadline ≤ now, or whose shift start ≤ now, becomes EXPIRED.

6. Scenarios

Chapter 8 defines a scenario as a real-life example of how the system is used, with a starting situation, a normal flow, what can go wrong, other concurrent activities and the state at the end. The scenarios below use the demo store of Section 2.3. Unless stated otherwise, the demo clock starts at Monday 2026-09-28 09:00 and the policy is EXCUSED. Each alternative sequence is written as a separate scenario, as Chapter 8 recommends.

S1. Lee Seoyeon requests a substitute for her midterm exam (UC-04)

Starting situation. Lee Seoyeon works Monday and Wednesday 18:00–23:00. She has a midterm exam on Wednesday 2026-09-30 and cannot work that evening.

Normal flow.

  1. Seoyeon signs in and opens the weekly board of the week of 2026-09-28. Her shifts are shown in blue.
  2. She opens her Wednesday 30 September 18:00–23:00 shift and chooses Request substitute.
  3. She enters the reason “Midterm exam” and the deadline 2026-09-29T21:00, and submits.
  4. The system checks BR-03: the shift is hers, SCHEDULED, in the future, has no open request, and the deadline is before the shift start.
  5. The system applies BR-01. Choi Minho has no shift on Wednesday and is available Wednesday 17:00–23:00, which covers 18:00–23:00. Kang Doyun has no shift on Wednesday and is available Wednesday 18:00–23:00. Jung Hana is not available on Wednesday. Both Minho and Doyun are eligible.
  6. The system stores the request as REQUESTED, two PENDING targets, and a REQUEST_RECEIVED notification for each of the two candidates.
  7. Seoyeon sees “Notified: Choi Minho, Kang Doyun”. On the weekly board her Wednesday shift now has a dashed amber outline.

What can go wrong. If Seoyeon enters a deadline after 2026-09-30 18:00, the system refuses and says that the deadline must be no later than the shift start.

End state. One open request with two candidates. This is the state of the demo data.

S2. Choi Minho accepts first; Kang Doyun is refused (UC-05)

Starting situation. The end state of S1. Both candidates have an unread notification.

Normal flow.

  1. Minho signs in. The header shows one unread notification. He opens the inbox.
  2. The item shows Seoyeon's Wednesday shift, the reason and the deadline, with Accept and Decline. He chooses Accept (two clicks from the header).
  3. In one transaction the system reads the request status (REQUESTED), sets it to ACCEPTED with Minho as acceptor, sets Minho's target to ACCEPTED and Doyun's target to CLOSED.
  4. Seoyeon and the owner receive REQUEST_ACCEPTED; Doyun receives TARGET_CLOSED.
  5. Doyun signs in and opens the inbox. The request item shows “Already taken by Choi Minho” and no Accept button.

Concurrent activity. If Doyun had pressed Accept at almost the same moment as Minho, his transaction would read the status after Minho's transaction committed. The status would be ACCEPTED, not REQUESTED, so his acceptance is refused with the same message (BR-02, NFR-04, TC-05x).

End state. Request ACCEPTED, acceptor Choi Minho. The shift still belongs to Seoyeon until the owner decides.

S3. The owner approves the swap and sees Minho's hours change from 16 to 21 (UC-06, UC-08)

Starting situation. The end state of S2.

Normal flow.

  1. Park Jiyoung signs in as owner and opens Approvals. The request of Seoyeon is listed as ACCEPTED by Minho.
  2. She opens it. The comparison panel shows:
    Table 20. Approval panel for S3 (week of 2026-09-28, policy EXCUSED)
    WorkerContract h (before → after)Scheduled h this weekHoliday allowanceHoliday h
    Lee Seoyeon (requester)10 → 1010 → 5Not eligible → not eligible (contract hours below 15)0 → 0

  3. She chooses Approve. The shift's worker becomes Minho; its original worker is recorded as Seoyeon; the request becomes APPROVED.
  4. The system recomputes both weekly summaries. Minho's holiday allowance for the week remains 16 / 40 × 8 = 3.2 h, that is 3.2 × 10,320 = KRW 33,024, provided he works all his shifts of the week.
  5. Seoyeon and Minho receive REQUEST_APPROVED.
  6. On the weekly board, the Wednesday shift has an amber left edge and stripe pattern, shows “Choi Minho” in bold and “Lee Seoyeon” struck through beneath it.

What can go wrong. If the owner chooses Reject, the request becomes REJECTED, the shift stays with Seoyeon and both receive REQUEST_REJECTED. Seoyeon then has to request again or work the shift.

End state. Minho holds the Wednesday shift; the weekly summaries are current.

S4. A request expires at its deadline (UC-11)

Starting situation. The end state of S1: the request is REQUESTED with deadline 2026-09-29T21:00. In this variant neither candidate answers.

Normal flow.

  1. The demo clock is advanced from Monday 09:00 with +1 day to Tuesday 2026-09-29 09:00. The request is still REQUESTED.
  2. The clock is advanced with +1 hour until it reaches 2026-09-29T21:00.
  3. On the next screen change the System Clock runs the expiry check. The deadline ≤ now, so the request becomes EXPIRED and both PENDING targets become CLOSED (BR-12).
  4. Seoyeon and the owner receive REQUEST_EXPIRED. The inbox items of Minho and Doyun no longer show Accept.

What can go wrong. The same happens if the request is ACCEPTED but the owner does not decide before the deadline. The owner then sees the request disappear from the ACCEPTED list.

End state. The Wednesday shift stays with Seoyeon. She may create a new request, because no open request exists for the shift any more, as long as the new deadline is before 2026-09-30 18:00.

S5. Jung Hana asks for a Sunday substitute and nobody is eligible (UC-04, FR-08)

Starting situation. Demo clock Monday 2026-09-28 09:00. Jung Hana cannot work Sunday 2026-10-04 10:00–16:00.

Normal flow.

  1. Hana requests a substitute for that shift.
  2. The system applies BR-01. No worker has an availability slot on Sunday, so the candidate list is empty.
  3. The request is stored as REQUESTED. The owner receives a NO_CANDIDATE notification. Hana sees the message that no eligible worker exists.

End state. The owner now knows, without any telephone call, that the shift has to be solved by other means. If nothing changes, the request expires at its deadline as in S4.

S6. Month-end payroll for September 2026 (UC-09)

Starting situation. The owner has finished the month and opens Payroll for 2026-09. The store has 4 regular employees.

Normal flow.

  1. The owner chooses Generate draft. The system computes one row per worker.
  2. Premium pay is 0 for every worker, because the store has fewer than 5 regular employees (BR-08).
  3. Holiday pay counts the weeks whose Sunday falls in September. For the week of 2026-09-21 (Sunday 2026-09-27), all shifts have confirmed attendance. Minho is eligible (16 h contract) and receives KRW 33,024 for that week. Seoyeon (10 h), Hana (12 h) and Doyun (12 h) are below 15 h and receive no holiday pay for it.
  4. Kang Doyun's wage is KRW 10,000. BR-10 does not apply to him: his contract (2026-08-15 to 2026-11-15) is shorter than one year and his work is simple labor. His applicable minimum is therefore the full KRW 10,320. His row shows a minimum-wage warning (BR-09).
  5. For Jung Hana the system checks BR-10: her contract is open-ended, 2026-09 is within three months of her contract start 2026-09-01 and before her probation end 2026-11-30, and her work is not simple labor. The reduced minimum 10,320 × 0.9 = KRW 9,288 applies. Her wage of KRW 10,320 is above it, so there is no warning.
  6. Rows that include shifts without recorded attendance use scheduled hours and are flagged “estimated” (BR-11).
  7. The owner tries Confirm. The system blocks it until the warning on Doyun's row is acknowledged. The owner acknowledges it and confirms. All rows become CONFIRMED and read-only.

What can go wrong. The warning does not change the amount; the system still pays Doyun's contract wage. The owner has to correct the wage in UC-01 and regenerate the draft before confirming if the error is to be fixed in this payroll.

End state. Confirmed payroll for September 2026.

S7. Policy variant ABSENT: a handed-over shift breaks perfect attendance (UC-10, UC-06)

Starting situation. The owner changes the substitution-attendance policy in Settings from EXCUSED to ABSENT. Choi Minho asks for a substitute for his Saturday 2026-10-03 12:00–18:00 shift.

Normal flow.

  1. BR-01 selects Lee Seoyeon (available Saturday 10:00–22:00, no Saturday shift) and Jung Hana (available Saturday 10:00–18:00, no Saturday shift). Kang Doyun is not eligible because his own Saturday 10:00–16:00 shift overlaps.
  2. Hana accepts.
  3. The owner opens the approval panel. Minho's contractual hours stay 16 and his scheduled hours fall from 16 to 10. Under the ABSENT policy the given-away shift breaks his perfect attendance, so his eligibility changes from eligible to not eligible. The panel highlights this line. Hana's scheduled hours rise from 12 to 18; her contract is 12 h, so she is not eligible before or after.
  4. The owner can now decide with this information. Under the default EXCUSED policy the same swap would leave Minho eligible, with 3.2 holiday hours.

End state. Depends on the owner's decision. The scenario shows why BR-06 is a setting and not a fixed rule (risk R1 of the proposal).

7. UML diagrams for requirements analysis

This section presents the analysis models. They are drawn in UML 2.5 notation [8]. Following Chapter 2, they show the system from several perspectives: the external perspective (use case diagram), the behavioural perspective (activity and state machine diagrams) and the structural perspective (analysis class diagram). Sequence diagrams belong to the design and are given in the final report.

7.1 Use case diagram

Figure 1 shows the three actors and the thirteen use cases. The Worker and the Owner are primary actors. The System Clock is a secondary actor that starts only UC-11. UC-04 includes UC-04a Find eligible candidates, which is the place of rule BR-01. UC-06 includes UC-08, because every approval recomputes the weekly summaries of two workers. UC-09 also includes UC-08, because payroll uses the weekly holiday allowance.

UC-11 and UC-12 extend UC-04. UC-11 applies at the extension point “deadline reached with no approved acceptance”. UC-12 applies when the requester withdraws the request. All use cases except UC-11 include UC-13, since a user must be signed in. The diagram contains the nine main functions of the proposal (UC-01 to UC-09) unchanged.

7.2 Activity diagrams

Figure 2 shows how candidates are selected when a request is created. For each worker the system tests the four conditions of BR-01 in order: active, not the requester, no overlapping shift on the date, and an availability slot that covers the whole shift. A worker who fails a test is skipped. After the loop the diagram branches on the number of candidates: with one or more candidates, targets and REQUEST_RECEIVED notifications are created; with none, the owner receives NO_CANDIDATE (FR-08).

Scenario S1 follows the first branch with two candidates. Scenario S5 follows the second branch. Scenario S7 shows the overlap test rejecting a worker (Kang Doyun).

Figure 3 shows the weekly holiday-allowance decision for one worker. The first decision is the 15-hour test on contractual hours. The second is perfect attendance: no shift of the week that the worker originally held and still holds may be ABSENT. For a shift the worker gave away by an approved substitution, the diagram branches on the workplace policy. Under EXCUSED the shift is skipped; under ABSENT it breaks attendance. If the worker is eligible, holiday hours are min(contractual hours, 40) / 40 × 8.

The diagram makes the policy variant of scenario S7 visible. It also makes clear that substitute shifts a worker took do not enter the 15-hour test, because that test uses contractual hours only.

7.3 State machine diagrams

Chapter 8 requires that states in a requirements model are domain-significant, that all sequences from the scenarios are allowed, and that prohibited sequences are not allowed. The three diagrams below are read against scenarios S1 to S7 with these rules.

Figure 4 shows the life of a substitute request. A request starts as REQUESTED. The first acceptance moves it to ACCEPTED (UC-05). The owner's decision moves it to APPROVED or REJECTED (UC-06). From REQUESTED or ACCEPTED it can also become EXPIRED (UC-11) or CANCELLED (UC-12). The four end states are final.

The diagram also shows what is prohibited. There is no transition from REQUESTED directly to APPROVED, so the owner cannot approve a request nobody accepted (BR-04). There is no transition out of ACCEPTED on a second acceptance, which is the state-machine form of BR-02. Scenarios S1–S4 each follow one path of this diagram.

Figure 5 shows that a shift is SCHEDULED when generated and becomes WORKED when the owner confirms attendance or ABSENT when the owner marks it so (UC-07). Reassignment on approval (BR-04) is a self-transition on SCHEDULED: the worker changes, the state does not. A shift that is WORKED or ABSENT can no longer be requested, because BR-03 requires a SCHEDULED shift in the future.

Figure 6 shows the two payroll states. A generated row is DRAFT. Re-generating the month replaces DRAFT rows. Confirmation is guarded: it is possible only when every minimum-wage warning of the month has been acknowledged (BR-09). A CONFIRMED row is final and read-only (FR-15), which scenario S6 ends with.

7.4 Analysis class diagram

Figure 7 shows the twelve domain classes, their attributes and their associations. Workplace owns Workers and the settings used by the rules (regular employees, substitution-attendance policy). A Worker has FixedSchedule entries, which define the contractual hours, and Availability slots, which feed BR-01. Shifts are generated from FixedSchedule entries. A Shift refers to its current worker and, after a swap, to its original worker. A SubRequest belongs to one Shift, has one requester, at most one acceptor, and one SubRequestTarget per candidate. Attendance is at most one per Shift. WeeklySummary and Payroll are derived records per worker and week or month. MinimumWage holds one row per year, and Notification belongs to one recipient and optionally one request.

The lower part of the diagram shows the five service classes (ScheduleService, SubstituteService, AttendanceService, PayrollService, NotificationService) and the LaborRules rule object. At the analysis stage each service operation stands for one step of a use case; for example, SubstituteService.respondToRequest realises UC-05 and SubstituteService.expireOverdue realises UC-11. LaborRules groups the business rules BR-01 to BR-12 as operations that read no stored data, so they can be tested alone (Section 9.3). Each domain class corresponds to one of the 12 database tables, so the data requirement of the course (at least four tables) is met three times over. The design class diagram of the final report will refine these classes with the sequence diagrams.

9. Requirements validation

Requirements validation checks that the requirements define the system that the customer really wants (Chapter 8). We check the five properties of Chapter 8 with the three techniques it names: requirements reviews, prototyping and test-case generation.

9.1 Requirements review

Two kinds of review are used. First, an internal review: the requirements lead's FR, NFR and business-rule tables are reviewed by the other two members against the problem statement and the scenarios. Second, a review with the owner of the reference store, in which the use cases are read out as scenarios in the owner's own terms and the owner is asked whether each step matches what they need. The owner's answers to interview question 6 (Section 3) decide the default of the BR-06 policy for the reference store; the system keeps both options in any case.

9.2 Prototyping

The screens of Section 8 form a working prototype on the demo data. The prototype is used in two ways. The team walks through scenarios S1 to S7 on it and records each step where the screen does not match the use-case description. The owner and the two co-workers are each asked to perform one task: the owner approves the request of scenario S3, one co-worker creates the request of S1, and the other accepts it from the inbox as in S2. We observe whether each task can be completed without help and whether the approval panel is understood. The results will be reported in the final report together with any requirement changed because of them.

9.3 Test-case generation

Chapter 8 recommends writing tests for requirements as a way to find requirements that are ambiguous or cannot be tested. We derived one test group per business rule before implementation. The test groups make three boundaries of the requirements explicit: the 15-hour boundary is inclusive (tested at 14.99 h and 15 h), the holiday hours are capped at 40 contractual hours, and shifts ending after midnight are counted with the end on the next day.
