1. Introduction

This interim report specifies the requirements of ShiftSwap and presents the UML models used to analyse them. ShiftSwap is a web application for a small store with part-time workers. It manages the weekly shift schedule, finds substitutes when a worker cannot work a shift, and computes weekly holiday allowance and monthly pay under Korean labour law.

The proposal (Week 5) defined the problem, the goals, the nine main functions and the project plan. This report refines them: problem and system environment (Section 2), elicitation (3), functional and non-functional requirements (4, 5), scenarios (6), analysis models (7), main screens (8), validation (9) and progress (10). Appendix A lists the test cases, which are fixed before implementation, and Appendix B the pages and the role-based access. The team follows the incremental model with prototyping of the user interface (Chapter 1) [6, 7]: increment 1 delivers the Must use cases and increment 2 the Should use cases. All identifiers (UC, FR, NFR, BR, TC) come from one project specification that the team uses as the single source of truth; the same identifiers appear in the models, the database functions and the tests. Identifiers are never renumbered: a requirement that was removed keeps its number unused.

2. Problem description and system environment

2.1 Background and current process

In small stores (cafés, convenience stores, restaurants) with fewer than ten part-time workers, each worker has fixed weekly shifts, for example every Monday 18:00–23:00. When a worker cannot come, the owner phones or messages other workers one by one to find a substitute. The owner then edits a spreadsheet by hand. At month end the owner must re-check each worker's weekly hours to decide whether the weekly holiday allowance (주휴수당) is owed.

Our reference store is the workplace of team member Hyewon Kim. The owner also acts as the manager. There, finding a substitute typically takes 3 to 5 contacts per request. The current process has four manual steps: (1) the worker informs the owner; (2) the owner contacts other workers one at a time; (3) the owner edits the spreadsheet and informs both workers; (4) at month end the owner re-counts hours and computes pay. None of these steps is recorded except the final spreadsheet.

Korean law adds rules that depend on these hours. A worker whose weekly contractual hours are 15 or more and who was not absent on any contractual working day of the week is entitled to a paid weekly holiday (Labor Standards Act Art. 55 ①, Art. 18 ③, Enforcement Decree Art. 30 ①) [3, 4]; in the labour ministry's view this does not apply to a week without a single day of actual work [25]. The 2026 minimum hourly wage is KRW 10,320 [1, 2]. Overtime and night premiums apply only in workplaces with five or more regular employees (Art. 11, 56) [3]. In such workplaces a part-time worker may work beyond the contractual hours only with the worker's consent and for at most 12 hours a week, and these hours earn a premium of at least 50 % (Fixed-term and Part-time Employees Act Art. 6) [20]; accepting a substitute shift usually creates such hours. The minimum wage may be reduced to 90 % during probation only if several conditions hold (Minimum Wage Act Art. 5 ②, Enforcement Decree Art. 3) [5].

2.2 Problems P1–P3

P1 Finding a substitute costs the owner repeated one-to-one contacts (reference store: typically 3–5 contacts per request).

P2 The schedule, weekly hours, pay and holiday-allowance eligibility of two workers change with every swap, and all recalculation is manual.

P3 Legal pay rules (minimum wage, holiday allowance, 5-employee threshold for premiums, overtime of part-time workers beyond contractual hours, probation reduction) are applied from memory, so errors are not caught before payday.

Scheduling products abroad already support a substitute workflow between co-workers. In Deputy a worker can offer a shift to co-workers who are qualified and available, and swaps can be set to require the manager's approval [10]. When I Work lets a worker offer a shift to all or selected eligible co-workers, subject by default to a manager's approval, and with its Find Replacement function a manager offers a shift to eligible workers, the first of whom to pick it up takes it [11, 12]. In Homebase a worker asks all or selected co-workers to cover a shift, and a manager gives final approval after one of them accepts [13]. Deputy's employee app is available in Korean, but managers who build schedules on its web interface must work in English [14], and in the help centres of all three products we found no mention of the Korean weekly holiday allowance.

Korean products follow a different workflow, in which the manager edits the schedule or an employee sends a change request to the manager. Shiftee lets an employee request a new or changed shift and computes the weekly holiday allowance automatically [15, 16]; its help centre states that the allowance is recognised only when every contractual working day is worked [16]. Shopl lets employees request schedule changes that the manager approves or rejects [17]. Albami and Gig let the owner manage schedules and the holiday allowance [18, 19]. The public feature descriptions of these four products do not include a substitute request between co-workers. We found no product that combines the co-worker substitute request with the Korean pay rules at the moment of approval, that is, one that shows the owner before the decision what a swap does to both workers' hours, holiday allowance and pay, to the store's labour cost and, in a workplace with five or more regular employees, to premiums and the 12-hour limit for part-time workers.

2.3 Proposed solution

ShiftSwap replaces steps (2) to (4). A worker creates a substitute request for one of their shifts. The system asks every co-worker who could work at that time, in one step, through the in-app inbox. The first colleague to accept becomes the acceptor; if every colleague answers “can't”, the requester is told at once to contact the owner. Before deciding, the owner sees what the swap does to both workers in that week — hours, holiday allowance and pay — and to the store's labour cost. On approval the schedule and both weekly summaries are updated. At month end the system prepares a payroll draft and flags minimum-wage problems before payday.

2.6 Actors

The system has three actors. A1 Worker (primary, human) is a part-time employee who views their own schedule, requests substitutes, accepts or answers “can't” to requests from co-workers and records attendance. A2 Owner (primary, human) is the store owner, who also acts as manager: the owner registers workers and fixed schedules, edits shifts and marks no-shows absent, approves or rejects substitutions, confirms attendance, reviews weekly summaries, confirms monthly payroll and edits settings. A3 System Clock (secondary, system) is a time-triggered actor. It ends open requests at their deadline (UC-11) and prepares the drafts of past months that have no payroll yet (UC-14). It has no process of its own: the database function run_system_clock() performs its duties whenever a screen is loaded, and the functions that answer, decide or cancel a request check the deadline themselves, so that no late action can succeed even when no screen was loaded. Its time is the demo clock while it is set, otherwise the database server time in Korea Standard Time.

4.3 Business rules

The business rules are the domain requirements (Chapter 8) that several functional requirements share. They are stated once here and referenced by number; each one is implemented by the database functions and tested by the test group of Appendix A.

Table 10. Business rules
IDRule
BR-01Eligible candidates. A worker W is eligible for a request on shift S if and only if W is an active worker, W is not the requester, the date of S lies within W's contract period, and no shift of W overlaps S on the time line of BR-05 (so a shift that ends after midnight on the previous day counts). Every eligible co-worker is asked.
BR-02First acceptance wins. The accept function changes the request with one statement, UPDATE … SET status = 'ACCEPTED', acceptor = W WHERE id = R AND status = 'REQUESTED' AND deadline > now AND shift start > now. If it changes no row, the acceptance is refused (“already taken” when another candidate is the acceptor, otherwise “request closed”). The candidate must hold a pending target and must not have an overlapping shift; the overlap is checked again at approval. On success all other pending targets are closed.
BR-03Request validity. Only the shift's current worker can request; the shift must be SCHEDULED and start in the future; at most one open (REQUESTED or ACCEPTED) request per shift; the deadline must be after now and not later than the shift start.
BR-04Approval effect. Approve: the shift's worker becomes the acceptor, the shift keeps its first worker as original worker, and the request becomes APPROVED. Reject: the request becomes REJECTED and the shift is unchanged. Only an ACCEPTED request can be decided, and only before its deadline and shift start.
BR-05Time line and week. A shift occupies the interval from its date and start time to its end time on the same date, or on the next date when the end time is not later than the start time. A shift lasts between 30 minutes and 16 hours. It belongs to the date and the ISO week (Monday 00:00 to Sunday 24:00) on which it starts, even when it ends after midnight. Two shifts overlap if and only if their intervals overlap; fixed shifts are compared on the weekly time line in the same way (Monday 22:00–02:00 overlaps Tuesday 01:00–05:00).
BR-06Holiday-allowance eligibility (주휴). W is eligible for an ISO week if and only if (a) W's weekly contractual hours C, the sum of W's fixed-schedule hours, are at least 15, (b) perfect attendance: no shift of the week that W originally held and still holds is ABSENT, and (c) W works on at least one day of the week: at least one shift of the week that W holds, including a substitute shift W took, is not ABSENT. A shift that has not been marked ABSENT counts as worked, so the owner only has to mark no-shows; the result for the current week is a projection. A shift that W gave away by an approved substitution counts by the workplace policy: EXCUSED (default; with the owner's approval W no longer owes work on that day, so the day is not an absence and attendance is not broken) or ABSENT (attendance is broken). Shifts W took as a substitute do not change C. When W is not eligible, the reasons shown are the contract under 15 hours for (a), each absent day and, under ABSENT, each handed-over day for (b), and “no day worked” (NO_DAY_WORKED) for (c), the last only when (b) holds. (Labor Standards Act Art. 55 ①, Art. 18 ③, Enforcement Decree Art. 30 ① [3, 4]; the law averages 15 hours over four weeks, and the fixed schedule is constant, so the average equals C. The labour ministry defines perfect attendance as no absence on a contractual working day on which work is owed and absence as not working on such a day of the worker's own accord, does not count lateness, early leave, holidays, leave or business suspension (휴업) as absences, and holds that the employer need not grant a weekly paid holiday for a week without a single day of actual work, even when every day counts as attended [25]. None of the quoted interpretations addresses a shift handed over to a co-worker: EXCUSED applies the definition of absence, (c) applies the last interpretation by analogy to a week whose shifts were all handed over and is a fixed rule, and ABSENT remains a setting.)
BR-07Holiday-allowance hours. Hours = min(C, 40) / 40 × 8, rounded to two decimals; weekly holiday pay = hours × hourly wage, rounded down to the won.
BR-08Premium pay. Applies only if the workplace has five or more regular employees (Labor Standards Act Art. 11, 56 [3]; Fixed-term and Part-time Employees Act Art. 3 ②, Art. 6, Enforcement Decree Art. 2 and Annex 1 [20]). Then +50 % of the wage for (i) hours of a work date beyond 8, (ii) hours of an ISO week beyond 40 not counted in (i), (iii) night hours, that is the part of a shift's interval between 22:00 and 06:00 of the next day on the time line (a shift 22:00–02:00 has 4 night hours), and (iv) for a part-time worker, hours beyond the weekly contractual hours not counted in (i) or (ii). The night premium is added to the others.
System rules for (iv), design decisions where the law does not fix the method: (a) a worker with 0 < C < 40 is part-time; (b) the test is weekly against C; (c) the hours of a week are taken in time order, and every hour after the first C hours is beyond contract; (d) an hour that meets more than one of (i), (ii) and (iv) counts once; (e) the night premium is separate. In a monthly payroll the days of a week outside the month only decide where the first C (and the first 40) hours end. The worker's consent is outside the system; the 12-hour limit is checked before approval (BR-15).
BR-09Minimum-wage check. Warn if the hourly wage is below the minimum of the year, multiplied by 0.9 if BR-10 applies. The payroll still uses the worker's wage; the warning blocks confirmation of the month until the owner acknowledges it. Re-generating a draft requires the acknowledgement again.
BR-10Probation reduction. The minimum may be reduced to 90 % only if all hold: a contract of at least one year (2026-09-01 to 2027-08-31 counts as one year) or an open-ended contract, a date within three months of the contract start and not after the probation end, and work that is not simple labour (Minimum Wage Act Art. 5 ②, Enforcement Decree Art. 3 [5]). In a monthly payroll the conditions are evaluated on the first day of the month, or on the contract start if later.
BR-11Monthly payroll. A worker gets a row for the month if they have a paid shift or holiday pay in it, or if they are active and their contract overlaps the month. Hours are summed in minutes and converted once. Base pay = minutes of the month's shifts × wage / 60, rounded down: WORKED shifts with their recorded times, SCHEDULED shifts with their scheduled times (the row is then flagged estimated), ABSENT shifts with zero. Holiday pay = sum of the weekly holiday pay of the weeks whose Sunday falls in the month. Premium pay = premium minutes × wage × 0.5 / 60, rounded down. Total = base + holiday + premium.
BR-12Expiry. An open request whose deadline or shift start has passed becomes EXPIRED; pending targets are closed and the requester and the owner are notified. This is evaluated whenever a screen is loaded, and the functions that answer, decide or cancel a request check the deadline themselves, so a late action is refused even if no screen was loaded; the expiry is then stored by the next screen load.
BR-13No taker. A request ends as FAILED when it has no eligible candidate at creation, or when every target has answered “can't”. The requester and the owner are notified; the owner's only action is to acknowledge. A FAILED request cannot be accepted.
BR-14Automatic payroll drafts. When the System Clock runs, every month from the first contract month to the month before now that has no payroll row gets a DRAFT computed as by FR-15, and the owner is notified once per month. No row is overwritten and nothing is confirmed.
BR-15Part-time weekly limit warning. With five or more regular employees and a part-time acceptor (0 < C < 40), let S be the acceptor's scheduled hours in the week of the shift after the swap. If S − C > 12, the approval screen shows a warning with S − C (“13 h beyond contract, limit 12 h”); exactly 12 hours gives no warning. The warning does not block the approval (Fixed-term and Part-time Employees Act Art. 6 [20]).
BR-16Swap cost estimate. For an accepted request on shift S in week W, the approval preview computes, for the requester and the acceptor, the week's pay before and after S moves from the requester to the acceptor: base pay (scheduled minutes of the shifts not marked ABSENT × wage / 60), premium pay (BR-08 applied to the same shifts of W, zero below five regular employees) and holiday pay (BR-06 and BR-07 under the workplace policy). The change of each worker is “after − before” per component, and the change in the store's labour cost is the sum of both workers' changes.
BR-17Attendance plausibility. Attendance is recorded only after the shift's scheduled end. Clock-in is placed on the day on which it falls within two hours before the scheduled start and four hours after the scheduled end, and clock-out after it (on the next day when it is not later than the clock-in); the recorded interval must lie within that range and last between 30 minutes and 16 hours. A 21:30 clock-in on a 22:00–02:00 shift is accepted; 23:00 to 18:00 on an 18:00–23:00 shift is not.

A worker who fails a test is skipped. After the loop the diagram branches on the number of candidates: with one or more, one target and one notification per candidate are created; with none, the request ends as FAILED and the requester and the owner are notified. The three diagrams below are read against scenarios S1 to S11 with these rules. Each transition is labelled with the database function that makes it; no other path changes the state. Expiry is made by expire_overdue_requests(), which the System Clock function run_system_clock() calls whenever a screen is loaded.

The analysis model uses the two kinds of classes of the course's object-oriented analysis: entity classes for the data of the domain and control classes for the use cases. To keep the diagrams readable, the entity classes and the control classes are drawn in two figures; the boundary classes are the pages of Section 8.

Figure 9 shows the eleven entity classes and their associations: a Workplace has Workers; a Worker has FixedSchedule entries, which define the contractual hours; Shifts are generated from FixedSchedule entries and refer to their current worker and, after a swap, to their original worker; a SubRequest belongs to one Shift, has one requester, at most one acceptor and one SubRequestTarget per candidate; Attendance is at most one per Shift; WeeklySummary and Payroll are derived records per worker and week or month; MinimumWage holds one row per year; a Notification belongs to one recipient and optionally to one request. The enumerations give the states of Section 7.3. Each entity class corresponds to one database table, so the system has eleven tables, above the course minimum of four.

Figure 10 shows the five control classes, which group the steps of the use cases: ScheduleControl (UC-01, UC-03), SubstituteControl (UC-04 to UC-06, UC-11, UC-12), AttendanceControl (UC-07), PayControl (UC-08, UC-09, UC-14) and AdminControl (UC-10, UC-13). The dependencies show which entities each one changes; ScheduleControl, SubstituteControl and AttendanceControl use PayControl to recompute a week after marking a shift absent, approving a swap or confirming attendance (UC-06 «include» UC-08). In the design each control operation becomes one database function, so the pages never change an entity directly.

7.5 Analysis sequence diagram

Figure 11 shows the case that the proposal feedback asked about: two candidates press Accept on the same request at the same moment. Each press is one call of the accept function and one transaction. The first call changes the request with its conditional statement, closes the other targets, notifies the requester and the owner, and commits. The second call waits for the row until the first transaction ends; PostgreSQL then evaluates its condition again on the changed row [9], finds the status no longer REQUESTED, changes no row and refuses with “Choi Minho accepted first”. No step runs in the browser between reading and writing the request, so two acceptances can never both succeed (NFR-04).
